Torsion spring wind-up toy

June 2025

Project Overview

A small car powered by a spring you wind with a key. Inside the shell, the key shaft winds a custom spiral spring, and a 34-tooth ratchet drive gear, a pawl carrier, and a 14-tooth pinion carry that stored energy to the wheels. I designed it from concept to printed parts and tuned the fits through several rounds of fit and function testing.

Spring analysis

I analyzed the spiral spring to check how far it can be wound before it yields. The spring is a 1.0 mm thick, 8 mm wide strip about 247 mm long, coiled about 4 turns between a hub with a D-shaped bore and an eye that pins to the chassis. I compared a beam theory hand calculation against a nonlinear static analysis in Ansys Mechanical, using PETG data sheet properties (E = 2020 MPa, yield strength 50.4 MPa).

01 · HAND CALC

Beam theory

Measured the strip in Fusion 360 and treated it as a beam in pure bending. That predicts a constant stiffness of 5.45 N·mm/rad and a bending stress of σ = E·t·θ / (2L), which reaches yield after about 2.0 turns.

02 · FEA SETUP

Nonlinear static model

Rotated the D-bore with a rigid remote displacement and held the eye, with large deflection on. Swept the mesh to keep 2 elements through the 1 mm thickness while staying under the student license node limit (67k nodes).

03 · SENSITIVITY

Fixed vs. pinned eye

Ran the eye both fixed and free to pivot on its pin. Fixed gave a constant 6.01 N·mm/rad (10% stiffer than beam theory, between the beam and plate limits). Pinned started 18% softer and stiffened as it wound.

What I learned: the hand calc gets the stiffness right but underestimates the peak stress, because the eye pushes back with a force as well as a moment, so the outer coil carries more bending than the beam formula assumes. That lowers the safe wind from about 2.0 turns to about 1.3. I first suspected a stress concentration where the strip meets the hub, but a fillet test in Fusion showed the strip already leaves the hub almost tangent, and the stress plot confirmed the hub region is lightly loaded.

Limits: printed PETG treated as isotropic with data sheet properties; coil to coil contact not modeled; the yield estimate extrapolates linearly beyond the solved range (up to 64°).
